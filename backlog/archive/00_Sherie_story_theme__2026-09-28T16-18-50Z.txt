The below was a discussion with a musical composer for Sherie's story theme called Kyrie Eleison. Do not interpret it literally, but just try to understand what the author is going for, for Sherie's personal role and story in Magiarchy. And adjust the site content relative to that. Your job is not to create a song, but to build her story around the author's ideas below:

I want **“Kyrie Eleison” (“Lord, have mercy”) to become a musical theme connected specifically to Sherie and her personal arc**.

Sherie is not a Magus and initially knows nothing about magic. She may only discover the supernatural world much later in the story, and that discovery could come with a serious personal cost. The connection between her and “Kyrie Eleison” does not need to involve magic directly. The important thing is that, if this piece exists on the soundtrack, its meaning should eventually become clear through Sherie’s character and story.

A good starting point is Sherie’s attitude toward relationships and manipulation, especially her line:

> “When men and women play games, the result is either cathartic or catastrophic.”

Sherie enjoys social games and knows how to use beauty, sexuality, charm, and psychological pressure to influence people. She is still one of the protagonists and fundamentally sympathetic, but she has a dangerous habit of treating attraction as a tool.

The music reflects this side of her. “Kyrie Eleison” is beautiful, dark, intense, and sexually charged. It is written in a deep D minor, moves at roughly 120 BPM, and uses multiple female voices in an almost operatic, repetitive structure. It should feel seductive and exhilarating while carrying an underlying sense of judgment, danger, and approaching consequences.

The thematic idea is that Sherie eventually has to **pay a price for repeatedly weaponizing her charm and manipulating desire**. Her behavior may lead her into a personal catastrophe, symbolic descent into hell, or some other form of retribution. The consequences should be serious enough that “Lord, have mercy” becomes emotionally meaningful in relation to her.

However, this should not turn Sherie into a villain or imply that sexuality itself deserves punishment. Her downfall should come from how she plays with people, power, desire, and consequences. She remains a side protagonist whose strengths and flaws are inseparable.

The desired mythological feeling is roughly this: **Sherie behaves in ways that Ishtar might admire, but eventually enters territory where Ereshkigal demands payment.**

Her arc should therefore contain:

- seduction and social manipulation as genuine forms of power;
- pleasure and excitement in playing dangerous interpersonal games;
- growing consequences that she initially believes she can control;
- a point where one of those games becomes catastrophic;
- some form of descent, punishment, sacrifice, or reckoning;
- a moment when the idea of “Lord, have mercy” becomes emotionally appropriate;
- and, importantly, the possibility of salvation afterward.

The goal is to find an original, professional, Type-Moon-inspired way to make this theme an important part of Sherie’s life or her role in the larger *Magiarchy* story, without making her a Magus or prematurely giving her knowledge of magic.
