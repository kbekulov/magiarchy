The purpose of this task is to make the project's English sound more recognizably written by Kiril Bekulov and less like prose generated, normalized, summarized, or maintained by an AI system.

1. Improve the existing prose where AI-like editorial habits have accumulated.

Treat the second part as essential. A one-time rewrite that leaves the current prompting architecture unchanged is incomplete.

## Core diagnosis

MAGIARCHY currently has a stronger problem with AI-like editorial and archive prose than with AI-like fiction.

The strongest Chapters and Moments already contain much of the desired authorial voice:

- concrete observation
- physical objects carrying meaning
- procedural thinking under emotional pressure
- dry humor
- embarrassment
- asymmetrical conversations
- tactical and institutional reasoning
- blunt language when appropriate
- short declarative sentences used selectively
- characters revealing themselves through behavior rather than psychological explanation

The weaker material tends to sound like an AI maintaining a highly organized knowledge system.

Common symptoms include:

- excessive use of words such as `now`, `remains`, `preserves`, `retains`, `establishes`, `clarifies`, `distinguishes`, `aligns`, `reflects`, `supports`, `records`, `ensures`, `current`, and `consistent`
- sentences describing what the archive does rather than what happened in the world
- excessive explanations of what a fact does not imply
- exhaustive qualifications intended to prevent every possible misunderstanding
- named editorial principles for observations that do not need formal terminology
- clean conceptual symmetry
- repeated contrast structures
- three-part lists used by habit
- generic dramatic concluding sentences
- psychological summaries that sound like test interpretation
- expert dialogue where every line efficiently advances the argument
- metadata written with more rhetorical polish than the underlying fact requires
- public prose describing synchronization, preservation, tracking, alignment, or maintenance
- paragraphs that explain their own significance after the scene has already demonstrated it

Remove these habits without flattening the project's intelligence.

## Target authorial voice

The archive should feel as though one intelligent, obsessive person maintains it because precision matters to him personally.

That person is strongly interested in:

- institutions
- hierarchy
- authority
- political leverage
- loyalty
- competence
- theology
- magecraft
- psychology
- failure under pressure
- strange supernatural rules
- tactical details
- physical objects
- awkward intimacy
- sexual embarrassment
- firearms
- clothing
- family inheritance
- moral responsibility
- absurd behavior during serious situations

Allow these interests to create recurring vocabulary and uneven emphasis.

Do not make the archive sound perfectly balanced.

Human authors have obsessions.

A minor physical gesture may receive more attention than an abstract theme. A joke may survive several revisions because the author likes it. One subject may be treated with unusual technical precision while another remains bluntly summarized.

Preserve that asymmetry.

Do not simulate humanity through deliberate grammatical errors, forced slang, random fragments, or artificial roughness.

The desired result should remain literate, precise, controlled, and professionally readable.

## Fundamental prose rule

Prefer this movement:

**object or action -> observation -> implication -> reaction**

Be suspicious of this movement:

**concept -> definition -> qualification -> contrast -> significance**

The second structure remains acceptable when genuine explanation is necessary, especially in technical or supernatural documentation. It should no longer dominate the archive.

## Reader-facing archive prose

Public archive pages should describe the fictional world.

They should rarely describe the archive itself.

Prefer:

- what happened
- what exists
- what someone knows
- what a rule does
- what an institution permits
- what remains unknown
- what consequence followed

Reduce language about:

- what the archive preserves
- what the record establishes
- what a page now includes
- what the system tracks
- what a revision aligns
- what a document supports
- what metadata distinguishes
- what the site ensures

Such process language belongs primarily in writer-facing maintenance documentation.

### Example principle

Weak archive voice:

`The current record now preserves the distinction between the two incidents and clarifies that no causal relationship has been established.`

Preferred direction:

`The incidents are separate. No causal relationship between them is known.`

Do not mechanically rewrite every sentence into this exact form. Apply the underlying principle.

## Qualification and uncertainty

MAGIARCHY requires careful distinction between:

- fact
- inference
- uncertainty
- contradiction
- reader knowledge
- character knowledge
- writer-only information

Preserve those distinctions.

However, stop explaining every interpretation a fact cannot support.

When one precise sentence can define the boundary, use one sentence.

Avoid chains like:

`This does not establish X. It also does not imply Y. No evidence supports Z. The relationship therefore remains unresolved.`

Prefer a direct statement of what is known and, where necessary, one direct statement of what remains unknown.

Uncertainty should feel factual rather than legally defensive.

## Character profiles

Review personality summaries, trait descriptions, connection summaries, motivations, conflicts, and timelines.

Reduce language resembling psychological test interpretation.

Prefer:

- habits
- characteristic mistakes
- recognizable reactions
- contradictions
- repeated interpersonal behaviors
- things the character notices
- things the character avoids
- situations that make the character lose composure
- characteristic ways competence fails
- private rituals
- practical preferences
- specific social weaknesses

Instead of describing a character as a clean psychological model, describe the pattern a reader could observe.

MBTI and cognitive-function material may remain writer-facing analytical support where appropriate.

Do not allow MBTI vocabulary to become normal narrative or character-profile English unless the field explicitly concerns typology.

A character should sound like a person before they sound like a model.

## Chapters and Moments

Do not perform an indiscriminate rewrite of current fiction.

The fiction is currently healthier than much of the surrounding archive.

Preserve:

- author-approved dialogue

- awkwardness
- character-specific banter
- procedural thinking
- object-based comedy
- blunt lines
- short declarative sentences
- uncomfortable silences

Concentrate fiction edits on:

- generic dramatic filler

- generic emotional summary
- suspiciously polished transitions
- repeated cinematic stingers
- dialogue where every answer arrives at exactly the structurally useful time
- exposition that proceeds with excessive Socratic efficiency

### Conversation inefficiency rule

Human conversation may temporarily fail.

Allow, where character-appropriate:

- ignored questions
- wrong interpretations
- interruptions
- irritation that delays the useful answer
- jokes that genuinely disrupt the argument
- repeated questions
- answers that come too early
- answers that come too late
- someone refusing the premise
- physical distractions
- conversational dead ends
- emotionally motivated repetition

Do not insert these mechanically.

Use them when they improve character truth.

A conversation does not need every line to justify itself through plot efficiency.

## Short dramatic sentences

Do not remove short declarative sentences merely because AI also uses them.

MAGIARCHY benefits from them when they are specific.

Strong:

`The organ had fallen silent. Felix had not.`

Strong:

`There was no pilot.`

Weaker tendency:

generic sentences about silence, darkness, tension, realization, fate, weight, or words "settling" when they could appear unchanged in unrelated fiction.

Keep stingers that belong unmistakably to this character, object, place, or situation.

Reduce portable drama.

## Required changes to AGENTS.md

Add a dedicated section titled approximately:

`## Author voice preservation`

It must establish the following rules clearly.

### 1. Current voice sources

For prose generation or revision, voice authority should follow this order:

1. Direct author-supplied wording and explicitly approved passages.
2. Current canon/default Chapters and Moments identified by the author as successful.
3. Current `docs/prose-style.md`.
4. Current character-specific speech and behavior evidence.
5. Other current reader-facing archive prose.

Historical versions are continuity and development evidence.

They are not automatically voice references.

### 2. Historical version quarantine

Add an explicit rule:

Do not mine historical versions, superseded Docs, old audit language, archived backlog prompts, or prior AI-generated summaries as a style corpus.

Use them only for:

- continuity
- provenance
- recovering author-supplied details
- comparing revisions
- identifying explicitly approved material

When old wording conflicts stylistically with current author-approved prose, the current voice wins unless the old wording itself was directly supplied or approved by the author.

This rule is important because the repository contains many accessible historical revisions and global search can expose them to future agents.

### 3. Public prose must describe the world

Add:

Reader-facing prose should primarily describe fictional people, events, rules, objects, places, institutions, and uncertainty.

Do not write reader-facing paragraphs about archive synchronization, preservation, validation, support, alignment, tracking, or maintenance unless the page itself concerns site administration.

### 4. No editorial certificate

Add a rule with this meaning:

After a scene, gesture, joke, image, or contradiction has communicated its implication, do not add a sentence certifying what it means unless the reader genuinely needs that information.

Trust demonstrated implications.

### 5. Behavioral characterization

Add:

Prefer observable habits, characteristic failures, choices, contradictions, gestures, and repeated social behavior over abstract psychological summaries.

Do not translate every character observation into MBTI, cognitive-function, attachment, systems, access, regulation, control, or optimization terminology.

### 6. Human asymmetry

Add:

Do not normalize every page into equal paragraph lengths, equal explanatory depth, equal rhetorical polish, or identical section rhythms.

Authorial emphasis may be uneven.

Preserve meaningful asymmetry.

### 7. Conversation inefficiency

Add the conversation rule described above.

Dialogue may contain interruption, misreading, repetition, avoidance, jokes, or temporary conversational failure when character-appropriate.

Do not optimize every line for explanatory efficiency.

### 8. Generic dramatic language

Add:

Be suspicious of dramatic sentences that could be moved unchanged into another fantasy, horror, thriller, or romance story.

Prefer sentences anchored to a particular object, rule, person, profession, place, or inconvenience.

### 9. Qualification discipline

Add:

State uncertainty precisely once when possible.

Do not list every conclusion the evidence fails to prove unless those distinctions are materially important to canon or the reader.

### 10. Editorial terminology containment

Add:

Craft terminology belongs in writer-facing Docs when it genuinely helps revision.

Do not propagate editorial terminology into Chapters, Moments, profiles, World pages, Home updates, or character dialogue.

### 11. No automatic style-rule accumulation

Add:

A successful sentence or scene does not automatically become a new universal prose rule.

Only add a permanent style rule when the author approves it as reusable or when several accepted passages demonstrate the same preference.

This prevents the style system from becoming increasingly bureaucratic after every revision.

### 12. Static-site tooling boundary

Add:

Repository scripts, npm commands, audits, generators, and verification tools execute locally during development.

Do not create prose or archive features that require Node.js, npm, a backend process, or a build server after deployment.

GitHub Pages receives committed static output.

If a development script generates a reader-facing file, regenerate and commit that file before publishing.

Do not assume GitHub Pages will run local development commands.

### 13. Do not over-build the editing system

Add:

Prefer a small transparent local script over a new package, framework, service, agent dependency, or external analysis system when a simple repository check is sufficient.

The prose-preservation system must remain understandable from the repository itself.

Future editors should be able to inspect the rule, script, and warning without relying on an external AI-detection service.

## Local automated style audit

Add a lightweight local repository script, for example:

`scripts/audit-prose-voice.mjs`

This script is executed by Codex or a human developer locally.

It is not loaded by the website and must not become part of the browser runtime.

It is a heuristic editorial assistant, not an AI detector.

It must never claim to determine whether text was written by AI.

Its purpose is to flag patterns associated with the repository's known failure modes.

Scan reader-facing textual sources and report suspicious concentrations such as:

- repeated `now` constructions in Home updates
- repeated maintenance verbs
- repeated `remains` sentence openings
- repeated `This does not establish`
- repeated `This does not imply`
- repeated `X now includes`
- repeated `X now preserves`
- repeated symmetrical sentence forms
- excessive occurrences of archive-process vocabulary within a short section

Do not fail a build because a single watched word appears.

Use frequency, clustering, or repeated sentence-pattern detection.

The report should show:

- file
- line
- matching phrase
- reason

Do not apply this heuristic aggressively to dialogue or Chapter prose.

Chapters should receive only the safest checks, such as known banned punctuation and obvious repeated stock constructions.

The script should require no external web service and preferably no new dependency if ordinary Node.js functionality is sufficient.

Add an npm command such as:

`npm run prose:audit`

This command is for local development.

It may be incorporated into the existing local verification workflow if doing so remains fast and reliable.

Do not alter the public deployment model merely to support this audit.

Stylistic findings should normally be warnings rather than hard failures.

Hard failures should remain reserved for actual project invariants such as prohibited punctuation or other explicitly mandatory rules.

The tool must not automatically rewrite prose.
