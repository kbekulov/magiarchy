# Kyrien’s origin, failed assassination, and recruitment

Develop Kyrien’s origin around this emotional trajectory:

**He trusted that an innocent person could obtain justice. After his father’s destruction, he spent years working to pay professionals who could prove what happened. Their work finally produced credible evidence, but the institutions capable of acting on it refused accountability. Then the public humiliation reached his already devastated mother, and he decided to kill the prosecutor responsible.**

## The officials responsible

The persecution is organized through **existing government officials linked directly or indirectly to the Magiarchy**, including the senior prosecutor and officials capable of influencing the prosecution, its oversight, and the treatment of Kyrien’s family.

Do not introduce a separate organization or faction called “hidden authorities.” Identify the responsible people through their actual offices, relationships, and decisions.

Knowledge is unevenly distributed. Some officials understand the magical reason for the operation. Others follow instructions framed as confidential national-security work, protect influential colleagues, or accept explanations they lack the access to question. Ordinary investigators and administrative staff can participate without knowing anything about magic.

The magical justification comes from the established suppression doctrine, with the Church holding final sanction and Magiarchal resources acting within that framework. Responsibility should remain traceable to particular decisions by particular people. 

## The father’s persecution

Kyrien’s father is a military-police commander. During the arrest and attempted escape of a fugitive, he intervenes to protect his officers. The fugitive assaults him; he survives and kills the fugitive in self-defence. During the confrontation, he unmistakably witnesses magic.

He tells nobody, including Kyrien.

Government officials connected to the Magiarchy learn what happened. His competence, professional credibility, and independence make them doubt whether his continued silence can be guaranteed. A senior prosecutor helps initiate a fabricated case intended to discredit him and force his submission.

Each successful rebuttal produces another allegation. The pressure eventually threatens his family. His father refuses to accept a false account of his conduct, despite continuing to keep the magical encounter private.

When the prosecution fails to make him controllable, the officials directing the operation obtain the necessary sanction to escalate. He is murdered, and his death is presented as suicide. The public account implies shame, instability, and fear of further revelations.

His death and public disgrace devastate Kyrien’s mother. She becomes increasingly unable to manage ordinary life, and Kyrien assumes much of her care. Preserve her individual habits, preferences, and occasional attempts at independence throughout the story.

## The years spent paying for justice

**Kyrien has no professional legal expertise. Lawyers perform the legal research, examine the evidence, pursue procedural remedies, and build the case.**

He believes the prosecutor and his associates destroyed his family, but still trusts that capable professionals can establish the truth and persuade the wider system to act.

He works relentlessly to afford those professionals while supporting his mother. Extra shifts, additional jobs, postponed ambitions, and repeated financial sacrifices keep the legal effort alive. Show the physical cost through ordinary details: attending a consultation after work, calculating whether another month of fees is possible, arriving home exhausted and still having household responsibilities.

His contribution is persistence. He hires representation, pays invoices, supplies family documents, recalls events, answers questions, and follows up when something needs clarification. He can recognize discrepancies involving his own family without independently mastering the law or uncovering an entire prosecutorial network.

The lawyers identify patterns across manipulated proceedings and coordinate the necessary professional work. Kyrien learns about developments through consultations, calls, and documents they explain to him.

Give at least one lawyer genuine competence and integrity. Their work achieves something substantial. The eventual failure should arise after credible evidence has been found.

Over time, the legal effort Kyrien finances helps trigger an independent inquiry into the senior prosecutor. A preliminary review confirms that important evidence against his father was knowingly distorted.

For the first time, Kyrien allows himself to believe that the years of work have purchased a real possibility of justice.

Then the inquiry reaches protected state interests.

Officials responsible for a closed review accept the prosecutor’s actions under a confidential national-security mandate. Those with Magiarchy connections protect the underlying reason. Others accept the restricted explanation without learning what it conceals.

The prosecutor is publicly vindicated. The inquiry becomes a supposedly malicious campaign against an honorable public servant.

Kyrien’s lawyers can explain what they established and why the available routes have now been blocked. They cannot make the state enforce consequences it has decided to prevent.

Someone Kyrien respects privately acknowledges that his father was wronged, but insists that the country cannot afford the consequences of pursuing responsibility.

**He has exhausted himself paying for the truth to be established. Now people with the power to act acknowledge enough of that truth and decline to do anything.**

Allow an interval after this defeat. He continues working and caring for his mother. His remaining faith has collapsed, but he has not yet decided to commit murder.

## The final breaking point

Some time afterward, Kyrien accompanies his mother to a neighborhood shop. She has recently begun attempting small errands again, and the visit represents a modest recovery of independence.

Someone recognizes her.

A customer repeats the public account: her family tried to ruin an honorable prosecutor for compensation or personal advantage. The accusation is casual and confident, delivered by someone who considers the controversy settled.

The allegation of financial opportunism has particular force after the years Kyrien has spent paying for representation.

His mother tries to defend her husband, but struggles to express herself. The shopkeeper, anxious to end the confrontation, asks **her** to stop making a scene.

Keep the encounter ordinary. One person watches; another looks away. Nobody needs instructions from the prosecutor. His vindication has supplied a public account that makes the family acceptable targets.

Kyrien intervenes and takes his mother outside. He concentrates on helping her and does not attack anyone or announce his intentions.

The decisive moment comes afterward, when she apologizes:

> “I’m sorry. I thought I could manage the shopping.”

She understands what happened as another failure of her own.

His father was persecuted and deprived of the standing to defend himself. Now his mother is being punished for belonging to the same family, and she has begun accepting responsibility for the punishment.

**After getting her home and attending to her needs, Kyrien decides to kill the prosecutor.**

Keep the contradiction present: his mother needs him, and his decision risks depriving her of the person still supporting her. He understands that risk. His ability to continue accepting the situation has nevertheless broken.

His outward routine continues. The emotional rupture leads into deliberate preparation, while his work, exhaustion, and obligations remain part of his life. Establish a credible arrangement for his mother’s care during his absences.

## The promotion dinner

The senior prosecutor is elevated to **Prosecutor General**. A formal dinner celebrates his appointment, presenting his survival of the supposed smear campaign as evidence of his integrity.

MSF assists with security through its existing role as a private contractor providing government security and investigative services. 

Lynleit attends. She privately dislikes the prosecutor but has no sufficient reason to display personal or public hostility. Their interaction remains professionally civil.

Kyrien attempts to assassinate him during the dinner. The attempt fails, partly because of the target’s concealed connection to the magical world. Kyrien witnesses nothing that reveals magic. Lynleit is nearly harmed, but he does not know she attended, and she conceals that fact during their subsequent encounter. 

He escapes immediate capture and leaves insufficient evidence for a conclusive case. He is later detained for questioning because of his history, motive, and possible connection to the incident.

Keep his practical intelligence and resourcefulness distinct from legal expertise. His competence during the attempt should have a credible basis without turning him into an infallible professional assassin.

## The interrogation with Lynleit

Develop the existing interrogation outline into a fully dramatized scene with dialogue, pauses, scrutiny, and negotiated terms.

Kyrien is exhausted, guarded, and worried about his mother. His years dealing with lawyers have taught him caution and the limits of what he understands. He follows advice he has received, avoids volunteering damaging information, and recognizes when a question invites an admission. Do not give him courtroom speeches or encyclopedic command of procedure.

Make Lynleit’s access to the questioning credible through the existing investigation. MSF’s involvement does not give her unlimited police or judicial authority. Account for legal representation and the privacy of her eventual proposal without making his lawyers conveniently disappear from the story.

Lynleit conceals her attendance at the dinner, her knowledge of magic, and her developing recognition of the suppression operation behind his father’s case.

Begin with controlled antagonism. She has legitimate anger toward someone whose actions nearly harmed her. He sees a well-connected person who can enter and leave the room while he remains confined.

Her competence appears through informed questions and particular observations. She becomes convinced that he was responsible, although her certainty exceeds what the available evidence can conclusively establish. Kyrien refuses to supply the confession that would close that gap.

She also discovers what his legal campaign actually cost him. Employment records, payment histories, and information from the case show years of ordinary work funding professional representation. His commitment becomes visible through what he sacrificed.

The central question is why he crossed the line when he did. The inquiry had already collapsed before the attack. Something happened afterward.

A possible dialogue anchor:

> “You were still paying your lawyers after the review.”
>
> “They were still answering.”
>
> “Then something changed.”
>
> Kyrien looked at the table.
>
> “What happened?”
>
> “They started on my mother.”

Let the scene earn that admission. He can acknowledge his family’s treatment without confessing to the assassination. His history should emerge through resistance, incomplete answers, and Lynleit’s existing knowledge.

As she learns about his mother, her understanding of him changes. Information she already possesses or can credibly access allows her to recognize the magical suppression behind the prosecution. She keeps that explanation private.

Give her a small practical response: she asks whether his mother has someone with her and arranges confirmation of her welfare. Kyrien answers this question more readily, then becomes wary of how much he has revealed.

Lynleit never threatens his mother. His existing circumstances already create pressure: continued detention would separate him from the person who depends on him.

Her recruitment motives remain mixed. She admires his competence, feels guilt over the injustice inflicted through the magical order, and senses an approaching danger for which she needs an ally outside her established circle. Preserve that intuitive warning from the existing outline. 

She offers help securing his release from immediate custody, protection against continued scrutiny, and a possible route toward a cleaner future in exchange for confidential service. Her influence has limits; she cannot promise unconditional immunity.

She reveals neither magic nor her full motives. Her stated position is practical: his abilities are useful, and cooperation offers his most credible route out of the situation.

Kyrien questions the arrangement, particularly its implications for his mother, his income, and his freedom. These obligations continue after recruitment. Lynleit’s offer must be materially possible for him to accept.

End with reluctant agreement. He recognizes her leverage and self-interest. She gains his cooperation without gaining his trust.

Lynleit subsequently tells only Felix and Reiner. Fionn remains outside the secret. Reiner trusts her judgment; Felix accepts her decision while continuing to distrust Kyrien. 

## The river and the prosecutor’s return

Kyrien remains completely unaware of magic throughout the origin, assassination attempt, interrogation, and initial period of service.

His first unmistakable exposure occurs during the later river incident, when he witnesses Lynleit walking on water. He tells nobody. She initially has only reason to suspect what he saw.

Preserve the parallel with his father: both encounter magic and remain silent. His father’s independence and capacity to threaten Magi contributed to the decision to eliminate him. Similar qualities eventually make Kyrien indispensable to Lynleit.

The Prosecutor General remains a recurring figure. His professional contact with Lynleit continues, requiring her to remain civil despite her dislike and knowledge of the family’s treatment.

Much later, after Kyrien openly knows about Magi and Lynleit knows he possesses that knowledge, the prosecutor commits a new transgression serious enough to bring him within the Magi framework’s punitive jurisdiction.

Explore his misuse of Magiarchy connections and suppression powers to control state leadership or remove officials obstructing his ambitions. Establish the precise prohibition before this accusation becomes central. Existing lore gives Magiarchs political interests and Church envoys political functions, so the violation requires a defined abuse beyond ordinary political involvement. 

Leave the prosecutor’s own magical status unresolved until separately decided, and ensure the eventual rule applies to him.

Lynleit deliberately offers Kyrien the possibility of revenge as an incentive. He recognizes the manipulation.

Her condition is firm: **they must establish a genuine violation serious enough to warrant the contemplated punishment.** His hatred, her dislike, and his family’s suffering cannot substitute for proof.

Keep the later offence separate from the original persecution. His father’s destruction was institutionally protected. The prosecutor’s eventual punishment may occur without anyone withdrawing that protection or admitting responsibility.

**Kyrien may finally obtain revenge from the same institutional order that still refuses to acknowledge what it owes his family.**
