# Kyrien: Family, Justice, and Recruitment

The author-confirmed origin behind [After the Failed Attempt v3](../story.html?chapter=after-the-failed-attempt&version=v3). This record separates the full history from what Kyrien and the reader know in the Chapter. The origin precedes his recruitment in early Arc 1 by years; its beginning has no assigned date or Arc.

## His father's persecution

Kyrien's father is a military-police commander. During the arrest and attempted escape of a fugitive, he intervenes to protect his officers. The fugitive assaults him. He survives and kills the fugitive in self-defence, unmistakably witnessing magic during the confrontation. He tells nobody, including his son.

Existing government officials linked directly or indirectly to the Magiarchy learn what happened. His competence, professional credibility, and independence make them doubt that his continued silence can be guaranteed. A senior prosecutor helps initiate a fabricated case to discredit him and force his submission. Each successful rebuttal brings another allegation; the pressure eventually reaches his family. He will not accept a false account of his conduct, but continues to keep the magical encounter private.

When the prosecution fails to make him controllable, the officials directing it obtain the necessary sanction to escalate. He is murdered. His death is presented as suicide, with public suggestions of shame, instability, and fear of further revelations. The [suppression doctrine](../church.html#suppression-doctrine-title) supplies the magical justification: the Church holds final sanction, and Magiarchal resources act within that framework. No named individual has yet been assigned the sanction or killing; do not assume Fionn or Mikhail ordered either.

Responsibility belongs to officials making particular decisions, not a new faction called hidden authorities. Some know the magical purpose. Others accept confidential national-security instructions, protect colleagues, or lack the access to question what they are told. Ordinary investigators and administrative staff need not know magic exists.

## The years he pays for justice

His father's death and disgrace devastate his mother. She increasingly struggles with ordinary life, and Kyrien takes on much of her care. She retains preferences, habits, judgment, and occasional attempts at independence. Her dependence is not her entire personality.

Kyrien initially believes capable professionals can prove the wrong and persuade the wider system to act. He works extra shifts and additional jobs, postpones ambitions, and repeatedly sacrifices money to support his mother and pay for representation. Work, consultations, invoices, and responsibilities at home leave him exhausted.

He has no professional legal expertise. Lawyers research the law, examine evidence, pursue remedies, identify patterns across manipulated proceedings, and coordinate the case. He pays, supplies family papers, remembers events, answers questions, and follows up. Recognizing a discrepancy about his own family does not make him an investigator who independently reconstructs a prosecutorial network.

At least one lawyer is both competent and honest. Their work helps trigger an independent inquiry into the senior prosecutor. A preliminary review confirms that important evidence against the commander was knowingly distorted. Kyrien permits himself to hope that the years of work have purchased a real chance of justice.

A closed review then accepts the prosecutor's conduct under a confidential national-security mandate. Officials with Magiarchy connections protect the underlying reason; others accept the restricted explanation without learning it. The prosecutor is publicly vindicated. The family's inquiry is recast as a malicious campaign against an honourable public servant.

The lawyers can explain what they proved and why the available routes are blocked. They cannot make the state enforce consequences it has chosen to prevent. Someone Kyrien respects privately admits his father was wronged, then insists the country cannot afford the consequences of pursuing responsibility. Their identity is not fixed.

There is an interval after this defeat. Kyrien keeps working and caring for his mother. His faith in obtaining justice has collapsed, but he has not yet decided on murder.

## The shop and the decision

His mother has begun trying small errands again. Kyrien accompanies her to a neighbourhood shop. A customer recognizes her and repeats the public claim that the family tried to ruin an honourable prosecutor for compensation or personal advantage. The casual accusation is especially bitter after years spent paying legal fees.

She struggles to defend her husband. The shopkeeper asks her to stop making a scene. One person watches; another looks away. Nobody has been sent to intimidate her. The official vindication has made the accusation ordinary enough for strangers to repeat.

Kyrien intervenes and takes her outside without attacking anyone or announcing his intentions. Afterward she says: "I'm sorry. I thought I could manage the shopping." She treats the humiliation as her own failure.

Only after getting her home and attending to her needs does he decide to kill the prosecutor. He knows that his mother needs him and that the decision risks taking away the person still supporting her. He continues his outward routine while preparing deliberately. Her care during his absences must be credible, and work and exhaustion must remain in his life.

## The Prosecutor General

The senior prosecutor is promoted to Prosecutor General. A formal dinner celebrates the appointment and presents his survival of the supposed smear campaign as evidence of integrity. MSF assists with security through its existing government contracts. Lynleit attends and remains professionally civil despite privately disliking him.

Kyrien's attempt fails partly because of the target's concealed connection to the magical world. Kyrien sees nothing that reveals magic. He escapes immediate capture and leaves insufficient evidence for a conclusive case; police detain him later because of his history, motive, and possible involvement. His practical resourcefulness has roots in his father's military contacts and instruction. It does not make him a flawless professional assassin.

Lynleit is nearly harmed without his knowing she attended. She never tells him. The prosecutor's connection to the Magiarchy is confirmed; whether he is himself a Magus is not. Do not restore the older description of him as a confirmed Magus politician.

## The interrogation and terms

Kyrien is exhausted, guarded, and worried about his mother. Years of legal consultations have taught him to follow advice, withhold damaging information, and notice questions that invite an admission. He does not conduct his own defence as an expert.

MSF's participation in the security investigation gives Lynleit access to questioning, not unlimited police or judicial authority. Legal representation remains active. Her certainty that Kyrien carried out the attempt exceeds what the available evidence conclusively establishes; he refuses to close that gap with a confession.

Employment records and payment histories show her what his legal campaign cost. She asks why he crossed the line after, rather than immediately upon, the inquiry's defeat. The author's dialogue anchor survives in v3: "You were still paying your lawyers after the review." / "They were still answering." / "Then something changed." / "They started on my mother." He can acknowledge the family's treatment without admitting the attempt.

She asks whether someone is with his mother and arranges confirmation of her welfare. She never threatens her. Continued detention already risks separating Kyrien from someone who depends on him.

Lynleit recognizes the suppression operation through information she can access. She conceals that recognition, the existence of magic, her attendance at the dinner, and her full recruitment motives. Admiration of his competence, guilt about the injustice of the magical order, and her intuition of approaching danger all contribute. Her suspicion of Helena remains grounded in existing questions about Fionn's movements and household, not foreknowledge of his assassination.

She offers help obtaining release from immediate custody, protection against continued scrutiny, and a possible cleaner future in exchange for confidential service. She cannot promise unconditional immunity. Income, care for his mother, and the possibility of leaving must be discussed. His family responsibilities survive recruitment. He agrees reluctantly, recognizing her leverage and self-interest; she gains cooperation without trust.

She subsequently tells only Felix and Reiner inside her established circle. Fionn remains uninformed. Reiner trusts her judgment. Felix accepts her decision without trusting Kyrien.

## Reader and character knowledge

Kyrien knows nothing unmistakably magical through the persecution, assassination attempt, questioning, or initial service. His father told him nothing. His first undeniable exposure is seeing Lynleit walk on water during the river incident. He tells nobody; she initially has reason to suspect rather than certain knowledge of what he saw.

Both father and son encounter magic and remain silent. The father's independence and capacity to threaten Magi contributed to his elimination; similar qualities make the son indispensable to Lynleit. Let the parallel emerge from their circumstances rather than turning it into a supernatural inheritance.

V3 shows the legal success and institutional refusal, the shop, and the interview. A separate Lynleit viewpoint recognizes suppression; it does not pass that knowledge to Kyrien. The father's actual magical encounter remains in the full reference history, not an event witnessed in this Chapter. See [Events, Witnesses, and Reveals](../docs.html?doc=reader-knowledge).

## Later revenge: confirmed direction and open rule

The unplaced Moment [The Prosecutor's Return](../moments.html?moment=the-prosecutors-return) carries this direction into Story and character scene listings without assigning it a phase.

The Prosecutor General remains a recurring figure. Lynleit continues to deal civilly with him in professional settings despite her dislike and knowledge of what happened to Kyrien's family.

Much later, after Kyrien openly knows about Magi and Lynleit knows he possesses that knowledge, the prosecutor commits a fresh transgression serious enough for the Magi framework's punitive jurisdiction. Lynleit deliberately offers Kyrien a possibility of revenge as an incentive. He sees the manipulation. Her condition is genuine proof of an offence serious enough for the contemplated punishment. Their hatred, dislike, and the family's suffering cannot stand in for that proof.

The offence and its prohibition are not yet selected. One development route is misuse of Magiarchy connections or suppression powers to control state leadership or remove officials obstructing his ambitions. Ordinary political involvement is insufficient: Magiarchs already have political interests, and Church envoys political functions. Define an actual abuse, establish the rule before the accusation becomes central, and make its jurisdiction apply regardless of the prosecutor's still-undecided magical status.

This later offence is separate from the protected persecution of Kyrien's father. Revenge may become possible without any institution withdrawing that earlier protection or admitting responsibility. Punishment is a possibility, not a completed event. No execution, new power, exact law, or timeline slot is established here.

## Draft choices and audit boundary

V3's unnamed lawyer, neighbour, tea, bread, payment details, interview blocking, and written employment terms provide concrete scene staging. They do not establish a named cast member, an exact salary, the date of the father's death, a new Narvean statute, or the identity of the official who sanctioned the murder. The neighbour is a familiar paid caregiver in this draft, not a resolution of the sister's circumstances or a permanent family arrangement.

The Nasu advisory audit focuses on the competent legal work achieving truth but not accountability, the mother's ordinary attempted independence, and the difference between later punishment and institutional redress. It preserves mixed motives and uneven knowledge without proposing another faction or treating coercive recruitment as attraction. Kyrien's legal and magical knowledge stay bounded. No physical capability score changes.

The exact attack choreography, people who authorized the original operation, later prohibition and jurisdiction, mother's continuing care arrangements, and later revenge timing belong in Questions. The author's new facts supersede the older all-powerful transfer-clause interpretation; they are not an unresolved contradiction requiring another decision.
