# Sherie: Kyrie Eleison

Author-confirmed direction, 28 September 2026. This gives Sherie's personal arc an intended shape, not a completed sequence of events. [Sherie's profile](../character.html?character=sherie) owns her established life; [Kyrie Eleison (Movement I)](../music.html#kyrie-eleison-movement-i) is her associated recording.

## The woman who enjoys the game

Sherie is a sympathetic side protagonist, a diplomatic apprentice with purposes of her own. She enjoys social play even when nobody needs persuading. Beauty, sexuality, charm and psychological pressure also give her real leverage. She likes discovering what will move a person and seeing her judgment work. That pleasure can encourage her to treat attraction as a tool long after the other person has stopped treating the exchange lightly.

Her [saying about catharsis and catastrophe](../docs.html?doc=character-aphorisms#sherie) belongs here. It is a risk she can recognize and still enjoy taking. Do not make her suddenly inept to produce a downfall: her successes should help explain why she believes she can manage a worsening situation.

## The promised cost

The intended arc moves through increasingly consequential games into one that becomes catastrophic. Sherie must pay a serious personal price for how she has used people, power and desire. The exact game, those affected, and the cost have not been chosen.

The descent may involve punishment, sacrifice, a reckoning or an experience that feels like hell to her. These are possible forms of the same direction, not four required events. Sexuality itself is not the offence, and a catastrophe must not turn her into a villain whose earlier warmth was all false. Her pleasure, competence, affection and manipulative habits can belong to the same person.

Eventually "Lord, have mercy" should become emotionally appropriate to her circumstances. It need not become dialogue, a Church ritual or a literal divine judgment. The soundtrack can make the plea audible while the scene gives her something particular to fear or lose. Salvation remains possible afterward; its means and outcome are open.

## Building the causal chain

The following is advisory development, not added canon. The Nasu audit favors a consequence she helps create over an arbitrary punishment delivered because she is seductive.

| Development task | What a future scene needs to establish |
| --- | --- |
| A successful game | What Sherie wants, whose desire she uses, and what the success encourages her to try again. |
| Escalation | A consequence she recognizes but still thinks she can contain. Let another person's aims continue outside her view. |
| Catastrophe | A traceable connection to her choices, without pretending she intended every resulting harm. |
| Reckoning | Something she cannot repair simply by obtaining the response she wants. Responsibility and vulnerability must cost her something concrete. |
| Possible salvation | A credible opportunity to act differently or receive help, without automatically cancelling the damage. |

Her diplomatic obligations offer an existing place for these pressures to meet the larger political story. No new mission, adversary, victim, bargain or rescue is assigned by this document. Choose the decisive events before creating a Moment or numbered timeline phase.

## What she knows

Sherie is not a Magus and initially knows nothing about magic. A much later discovery of the supernatural world is possible and may carry a serious personal cost. Neither the discovery nor its connection to her catastrophe is settled. Her competence in reading people does not let her recognize hidden magecraft without evidence.

The river operation can reveal magic to Kyrien and the reader without telling Sherie what happened. Her request for an investigation, access to officials and association with MSF do not grant her their concealed knowledge. [Events, Witnesses, and Reveals](../docs.html?doc=reader-knowledge#sherie-and-the-hidden-world) keeps these distinctions explicit.

## Felix is a person within this life

The [private-game direction](../docs.html?doc=character-intimacy-and-sexuality#sherie-and-felix-the-private-game) retains its affection, trust, discretion and uneven attachment. Sherie's genuine ease with Felix must not become a retroactive lie. Their shared play can reveal the difference between influencing someone and caring what happens to him, but he is not yet designated the victim, cause or savior of her catastrophe.

His fixation on Lynleit continues to matter. Her own flaws do not make his hurtful inattention deserved. Nor does his care supply an automatic cure for her decisions. Their ordinary company and professional duties need enough space that the reckoning threatens a life the reader has actually come to know.

## The sound and the mythic comparison

The musical brief is beautiful, dark, intense and sexually charged: deep D minor, roughly 120 BPM, multiple female voices, and an almost operatic repetitive structure. Seduction and exhilaration carry an undertone of judgment, danger and approaching consequences. These are supplied creative directions, not independently measured properties or in-world magical instructions.

The mythic comparison places Sherie between conduct Ishtar might admire and territory where Ereshkigal demands payment. Use it to think about power, descent and cost. It establishes neither goddess as a story actor, gives Sherie no Cult membership, and does not require a literal underworld. [Thematic Direction](../docs.html?doc=thematic-direction) owns that separation.

Kyrie Eleison is Sherie's personal theme. It is not automatically the unnamed recurring motif proposed for Sherie and Felix's relationship. A recording's title, artwork and sacred language do not decide her faith, magical status or fate.
