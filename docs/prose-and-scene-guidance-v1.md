# Prose and Scene Guidance

For narrative voice, dialogue, rhythm, comic timing, and a log of Chapter-specific style choices, use [MAGIARCHY Prose Style](../docs.html?doc=prose-style) alongside every draft. This document owns revision boundaries and development approaches.

This is editorial guidance. It does not add events, motives, or supernatural rules to canon. Established facts belong in their archive records; a Chapter lets the reader experience them.

## Revision priorities

- Keep the particular image, gesture, joke, or inconvenience. Remove an explanation when the scene has already communicated it.
- Do not follow every strong line with a stronger-sounding conclusion. Ordinary sentences give an interruption or startling image room to register.
- Keep interpretation close to a character's knowledge. A frightened or mistaken conclusion is useful; an omniscient verdict about everyone involved often ends the uncertainty too early.
- Preserve different speech habits. Felix can keep a joke going; Reiner can decline to participate. Natalia may pursue a definition while Lynleit wants an answer. Kyrien may answer plainly and fail to reassure anyone.
- Do not defend a competent character after every bad reaction. Competence, affection, embarrassment, selfishness, and poor judgment can coexist.
- Give each consultation a new piece of evidence, a failed treatment, a limitation, or a disagreement. Do not have several experts repeat one explanation in different vocabularies.
- Use MBTI to check a portrayal, not as its vocabulary. People do not need to think about their relationships as systems, access, or control merely because those terms helped an audit.

## Where information belongs

**Chapters:** action, perception, dialogue, and character interpretation. Keep later revelations out of an earlier scene's closing explanation unless a deliberate flash-forward is part of the intended structure.

**Wiki records:** identity, properties, history, limits, and known uncertainty. Describe what a coat does, not what preserving the coat achieves for the writer.

**Moments:** concise scene facts, continuity, and reader knowledge. A later consequence may remain in continuity even when it is removed from the Chapter itself.

**Editorial Docs:** alternatives, missing facts, unresolved contradictions, and proposed effects on the reader. An attractive proposal is not evidence that an event happened.

Character timelines use short summaries tied to named events. Biography carries the fuller history. Connections describe the relationship; conflicts state a specific unresolved pressure. No section needs generic filler to look complete.

## Preferred development approaches

These preserve earlier author-favored directions without making every unanswered question demand the same kind of answer.

### Institutions and conspiracies

Give factions defensible but incompatible aims. Several people can contribute to Fionn's death without sharing a complete plan. The frame against Lynleit can rely on true observations placed in a false causal order. Fionn's caution and Helena's exclusion should have concrete consequences rather than receive moral verdicts from the narrator.

The author favors old rules meeting unforeseen cases, limited Church authority with severe consequences, and MSF's mutual dependence with the state. Use these where an actual decision needs them. A rank, contract, or procurement detail can remain brief when no choice depends on it.

### Investigations and supernatural evidence

A small observation may distinguish Drake. Evidence that Heyk cannot make useful to an institution may explain his need for unconventional allies. Natalia and Lester can understand different parts of one case. Experts on the Spill may each possess only part of the explanation.

Separate a disappearance's mechanism, destination, and possibility of return when that preserves a worthwhile mystery. Keep uncertainty honest: if the author has not decided the answer, do not label it a conclusion the reader should infer.

### Places and domestic life

Earlier directions connect the park's vulnerability to its history, the house's old name to a personal memory, and the river's aftermath to a lasting change. A room may have a restriction learned through behavior rather than exposition. These are development directions, not permission to invent a history or supernatural effect.

Keep room for tea, laundry, clothes, the cat, and ordinary irritation. Such details do not need to be turned into symbols each time they appear. The relative entrusted with the child can be reliable without being someone Lynleit finds easy company.

### Relationships

Let repeated work, arguments, games, and ordinary contact establish the pair before a crisis intensifies it. Trust does not explain every attraction. Felix may come to understand Kyrien without liking him; Natalia's refusal of a case may say more than another successful examination.

Use a private gesture in the hotel refuge only if it fits Lynleit's immediate exhaustion. Do not insert a prescribed tender beat merely because the scene is important to the couple. Her duty and the main story remain primary.
