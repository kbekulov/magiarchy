# MAGIARCHY Prose Style

This is the working house style for Chapters. Its starting point is the author's preference for concrete scenes, unruly conversation, unsettling supernatural rules, and intimacy that intrudes on a story concerned with other things. It develops through actual Chapters rather than through imitation of another writer.

Use this document alongside [Prose and Scene Guidance](../docs.html?doc=prose-and-scene-guidance), which owns revision boundaries and development approaches. This document owns narrative voice and craft choices.

## Narrative voice and focalization

**Default:** third-person past tense with a clearly established focal character. Let the narration borrow that person's vocabulary and immediate concerns through free indirect discourse, without turning every thought into an italicized monologue.

Stay close during fear, embarrassment, discovery, and difficult decisions. A character can notice a wet cuff before understanding a curse. Widen the psychic distance for travel, repeated failed attempts, elapsed time, or a rule already established in the scene.

Change focalization at a section or scene break. In Doom Has an Address, Lynleit's research and Kyrien's incomplete account require separate access to their thoughts. Neither should know what the other has concealed simply because the narrator does.

The narrator may be dry, observant, and briefly amused. Comic commentary is welcome when it sharpens a particular absurdity. Avoid an omniscient verdict that explains what every gesture proves about the cast.

## Tonal register and counterpoint

The principal register combines precise observation with conversational ease. Technical language belongs to a character solving a technical problem; it should not become everyone's inner voice.

Let terror, ordinary inconvenience, and embarrassment occupy the same scene without cancelling one another. The fatal curse remains fatal while Lynleit objects to the proposed solution. A lost jacket can matter to her without being the emotional thesis of the river chapter.

Humor often comes from unequal seriousness: the answer is technically useful, socially unbearable, and delivered by someone enjoying that discrepancy. Do not make every character equally witty. Reiner need not return Felix's joke; Kyrien's plain question can cause more trouble than a clever retort.

The Nasu advisory framework may help test a rule, exception, or dramatic consequence. It does not supply a borrowed voice, stock catchphrase, or permission to imitate a particular author's prose.

## Scene, summary, and exposition

Dramatize the point where someone must answer, act, refuse, or reconsider. Summarize the travel and repeated research that bring them there.

A supernatural explanation earns space when it changes the available choices. Natalia's explanation of the ego is a discovery in this scene, not background terminology the narrator should give Lynleit beforehand. Mikhail contributes a different body of evidence before giving an embarrassingly practical answer.

Preserve the **discovery sequence**: observation, interpretation, objection, revised possibility. Let an explanation meet resistance. The listener may interrupt for the wrong reason, misunderstand a word, or resent a perfectly accurate answer.

“Show, don't tell” is a preference for dramatizing important experience, not a ban on exposition. State a mechanism plainly when withholding it would confuse the scene. Do not then explain its emotional meaning after the reader has already watched the reaction.

## Dialogue, idiolect, and subtext

Speech should carry at least one immediate purpose: obtain an answer, evade it, test a premise, provoke, reassure, delay, or end the conversation. The purpose need not match what the speaker admits.

### Lynleit

**Idiolect:** specific questions, impatient objections, attempts to return a discussion to its useful conclusion.

She can handle professional disclosure yet become evasive when the subject is personally embarrassing. Repeated denials may expose what she intends to close.

### Natalia

**Idiolect:** precise definitions, associative examples, a former teacher's questions, and dry corrections.

She enjoys making Lynleit follow a sound argument to an unwelcome conclusion. Do not remove her playfulness in order to make her seem intelligent.

### Father Mikhail

**Idiolect:** patient doctrinal explanation followed by unexpectedly blunt ordinary language.

He continues giving useful advice while Lynleit is trying to escape the conversation. His irreverence does not erase his conviction or severity elsewhere.

### Kyrien

**Idiolect:** short questions, literal observation, suspicion when someone's behavior changes.

He can recognize that something happened without possessing the missing explanation. Frustration does not require a confession or an eloquent speech.

These are observations from the current Chapters, not scripts for every future line. MBTI and function emphasis support a characterization check; they do not dictate syntax or replace personal history.

Prefer unobtrusive dialogue tags. Use action beats when they locate a speaker, change the timing, or reveal divided attention. A page turned without being read does more here than several descriptions of hidden desire.

## Comic timing and repetition

Distinguish **redundant exposition** from **comic repetition**. Repeated information can be cut; a repeated denial may be the action of the scene.

Preserve the setup, escalation, pause, reversal, and closing beat when revising a comic exchange. Read it aloud before removing a line.

In Doom Version 3:

- The examination routine restores the old teacher-student relationship before Natalia supplies the ego hypothesis.
- The academically respectable answer sets up the instruction to consult a priest.
- Mikhail's apparent consideration of centuries of theology delays an exceptionally blunt answer.
- His repeated use of Lynleit's name accompanies successive stages of her exit.
- “It worked” and “By failing” make the same recovery mean different things to the speakers.
- The later “I haven't asked anything” exchange recalls the earlier call. The repetition now exposes Lynleit's anticipation.
- Natalia's final question works because the preceding silence has made Lynleit keep talking.

Do not compress this into a summary that says Natalia teased her. Do not explain the punchline afterward. A joke may need several apparently unnecessary lines to establish its timing.

## Syntax, rhythm, and paragraphing

Use complete, varied sentences for the narrative baseline. Alternate longer sentences that follow observation or reasoning with shorter sentences at a decision or interruption.

Short paragraphs and fragments are emphasis tools. A whole chapter of isolated declarations gives every detail the same weight. Dialogue can remain one utterance per paragraph because the white space controls turn-taking and pace.

Use a deliberately repeated structure for a specific effect, then leave it. Avoid habitual three-part lists, mirrored reversals, and final sentences that restate the paragraph in grander language.

Keep tense consistent unless a quoted document or deliberate temporal shift requires otherwise. No em dashes.

## Diction and imagery

Prefer concrete verbs, recognizable materials, and specific physical relationships. Fishing line, a soaked sleeve, an unread page, and a notebook are useful because someone can touch or act on them.

Choose imagery through focalization. Lynleit may notice dress and composure; Kyrien may notice a change in someone's movements. Neither should become an all-purpose sensor reporting every detail.

Use an extended metaphor only while it helps the reader reason. Natalia's address metaphor makes the curse's recognition understandable; it should not spread into every description of affection, leadership, or domestic life.

Do not justify an appearance or defend a character after every flawed response. Do not label an ordinary feeling as profound. Leave room for awkwardness that remains awkward.

## Subtext, ellipsis, and reader knowledge

Subtext is what a reader can infer from speech, behavior, juxtaposition, or omission. It is not a fact the author has not decided yet.

Ellipsis may omit an encounter while showing its consequences. Doom Version 3 does not narrate the incomplete attempt itself. The next call and Kyrien's account establish what they can establish; exact choreography remains unresolved.

Mark Chapter preface and Moment conclusions as inferred only when the scene supplies evidence without stating the conclusion. If a revision makes the explanation explicit, change the label. Do not explain a purple inference in narration merely to make it easier to validate.

Keep later consequences out of an earlier scene's closing summary unless a flash-forward is a deliberate narrative choice.

## Terminology and researched allusion

Physicians are ordinary medical professionals without knowledge of magic unless the author explicitly establishes an exception. Do not invent a class of physicians treating thaumaturgy. A medical examination, if used, must preserve that ignorance and show or account for concealment. Doom Version 3 uses Magi's examination of the curse and does not add an off-page physician visit.

Natalia introduces the ego hypothesis and attributes the psychological definition to Jung. Her application of it to the curse belongs to fictional magecraft, not to Jung's claim. Brief quotations must be verified; store their source here rather than interrupting dialogue with research apparatus.

The short definition quoted in Version 3 is reproduced in the [C. G. Jung Society of Sydney's account of ego, shame, and embodiment](https://www.junginsydney.org.au/events/2023/egoshame). Only that definition is quoted. The curse mechanism and Natalia's proposed use of reciprocal change are fictional.

## Revision protocol

Before drafting or editing a Chapter or Moment, read this document, the chosen source version, and the relevant character records. Identify the focal character, discovery sequence, emotional pressure, and any dialogue whose timing must survive.

During revision, keep two separate passes:

1. **Dramatic pass:** continuity, knowledge, choices, voice, discovery, and the function of each exchange.
2. **Line-edit pass:** cadence, diction, repetition, dialogue tags, paragraphing, and unnecessary explanation.

Compare the result against the source. Restore any lost joke, disagreement, or character-specific awkwardness that was removed merely for concision. Recheck the preface, Moment, and F/M/S anchors against the actual revised paragraphs.

Afterward, add a concise entry below only when the Chapter teaches us a reusable style choice. Give its source, craft term, example or location, and limit. Separate author decisions from editorial working choices. Do not accumulate a new absolute rule for every attractive sentence.
