# Chapter 01: Before the First Bell

> Working draft based on the confirmed opening scene. Dialogue and exact staging remain provisional.

## 05:17

The first week of September had arrived without deciding whether it belonged to summer or autumn.

The city was dark when the MSF car turned onto Saint Orison Street. Rain from the previous night still rested in the joins between the stones, and the church at the end of the road seemed to have collected more darkness than the buildings around it. Its windows were unlit. Its bell had not yet marked the hour.

Felix slowed at the iron gate.

"This is the address?"

Reiner checked the card in his hand for the third time. "Unless the Church has opened a second Saint Orison Cathedral overnight."

"It would explain the budget."

From the rear seat, Lynleit looked past them toward the steps. She had said little since they left MSF headquarters. Her coat was buttoned neatly, her expression composed, and the file resting across her knees had not been opened once.

Felix and Reiner were present as MSF security, but neither belonged to her life as a mere subordinate. They were friends Fionn trusted as her personal bodyguards, familiar company on official journeys and casual outings alike.

Fionn sat beside her with his eyes closed.

"The east entrance," he said.

Felix glanced at the empty street. "Of course. Much less suspicious than the front."

Lynleit looked at him through the mirror. "Your gift for discretion remains one of the agency's better-kept secrets."

"Thank you."

"That was not praise."

"I accepted it before the correction."

Reiner hid a smile behind the route card.

They drove around the cathedral and stopped beside a narrow door set into the oldest part of the stonework. A priest was already waiting beneath the arch. He wore no coat despite the cold.

Fionn stepped out first. Lynleit followed, taking the file with her.

Felix reached for his door.

The priest raised one hand.

"The officers will remain outside."

Felix paused with the door half open. "We are their security detail."

"You are expected to wait."

Reiner looked to Fionn. "Director?"

Fionn did not appear surprised. "Stay with the car."

"Sir, if this is a negotiation, we should at least clear the room."

"The room has already been cleared."

The priest opened the door. Warm amber light touched the wet stones, then narrowed as Fionn entered.

Lynleit stopped before following him.

"Try not to investigate the clergy while we are inside," she said.

Felix leaned against the open car door. "How long will you be?"

"Long enough for you to invent three explanations and believe the least sensible one."

Then she disappeared into the church, and the door closed behind her.

## Outside

At 05:31, Felix decided the meeting concerned money.

At 05:38, he revised this to blackmail.

At 05:46, after a delivery van passed without stopping and a light appeared in one high window, he proposed that Fionn had joined a secret monastic order and intended to leave MSF to Lynleit before breakfast.

Reiner remained beside the car, arms folded.

"You have been specifically instructed not to invent explanations."

"She predicted three. It would be rude to disappoint her."

"You are already on four."

"The first was a working theory."

Across the street, a newspaper bundle landed outside a closed kiosk. The top page loosened in the wind. For a moment, Felix could read part of the headline.

SECOND VOLUNTEER MISSING NEAR VILEN PARKLAND

The page folded over itself before he could see the rest.

"They closed another path through the park," he said.

Reiner followed his glance. "Drainage works."

"In September?"

"Rain happens in September."

"People also happen in September. They usually do not vanish into landscaping projects."

Reiner looked back toward the church door. "You are supposed to be watching the entrance."

Felix sighed and obeyed.

The bell still had not rung.

## An Early Exit

At 05:53, the side door opened.

Lynleit came out alone.

She descended the steps with the same measured pace she used when crossing the operations floor, but the file was now held too tightly beneath one arm. Her expression was calm in the deliberate way that meant calm had required effort.

Felix straightened.

Reiner opened the rear door for her. "Is the Director following?"

"Eventually."

She did not get into the car.

Felix and Reiner exchanged a look. Lynleit noticed it immediately.

"No," she said.

Felix placed a hand against his chest. "I have not asked anything."

"You have asked several things. You simply used your face."

"Then my face would appreciate clarification. Why is the director of an intelligence foundation negotiating with a church before sunrise?"

"Perhaps he is confessing the budget."

"Reiner said money too."

"I said no such thing," Reiner replied.

Felix continued. "We were refused entry. The priest knew we were coming. You left early, and you look as though somebody inside suggested something very stupid with complete confidence."

Lynleit looked back at the closed door.

For one second, the irritation showed plainly. Not anger exactly. Something more contained, sharpened by disappointment.

Then she turned to them again, and the edge softened into intent.

"You are both remarkably interested in private church business."

"Occupational concern," Felix said.

"Of course. Should I ask whether the priest has time to arrange a ceremony for you? You are already dressed, and Reiner has spent the entire morning pretending not to enjoy your company."

Reiner went still.

Felix smiled. "Would MSF cover the reception?"

"Only if you classify it as a training exercise."

"I object to this entire conversation," Reiner said.

"Noted," Lynleit replied. "Felix, put his objection in the wedding file."

"We have a wedding file?"

"We do now."

The cathedral bell struck once.

The side door opened again, and Fionn stepped into the morning with the priest behind him. Whatever expression he had worn inside was gone before he reached the first step.

He looked from Lynleit to Felix, then to Reiner.

"Did I miss something?"

"Felix and Reiner are considering a church wedding," Lynleit said.

"We are not," Reiner replied.

Fionn considered this with the gravity of an official report. "Then I appear to have missed very little."

He entered the car.

Lynleit followed without looking back at the church.

As Felix pulled away from the curb, the morning traffic reports announced a new diversion around the park. The explanation was maintenance. The estimated reopening date was left unspecified.

Nobody in the car commented on it.
