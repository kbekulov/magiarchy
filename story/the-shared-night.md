# The Shared Night

The first indication that something had gone wrong was not screaming.

It was embarrassment.

By nine in the morning, three separate government departments had received complaints from people insisting that somebody else had entered their dreams.

By eleven, the number was twenty-seven.

By lunch, nobody wanted to put anything in writing.

A customs officer in Vilen swore that his supervisor had shot him six times inside a railway station that did not exist.

The supervisor remembered doing it.

Two university students separately described spending an entire night searching the same impossible library for a room numbered zero.

A married accountant refused to explain his dream after learning that his secretary remembered it too.

The secretary submitted her resignation before breakfast.

A retired woman on Orsin Street reported drinking tea with a neighbour she had barely spoken to in twenty years. The neighbour remembered the tea, the green cups, the rain outside and the name of the dead husband they had discussed.

There were no injuries.

No missing people.

No unexplained physical marks.

Only memories.

That made the incident worse.

By the third night, the phenomenon had acquired the provisional designation HI-009.

The following morning, Natalia stood beside the incident board with a stack of reports nobody wanted read aloud.

Natalia objected to the nickname everyone else had already started using.

"The Shared Night sounds like a romance novel."

"Nothing yet."

"I need evidence."

"I investigate crimes. Precise terminology matters."

"More than two."

"Three?"

"Definitions vary."

"Peripheral discrepancies. Colour. Distance. Sometimes sequence. One person remembers six windows, another remembers seven. But conversations, major actions and emotionally charged events remain unusually consistent."

"It is socially catastrophic."

"I meant magically."

"That too."

"The environments themselves appear composite. Memory contributes. Expectation contributes. So do fear, attachment, shame, resentment, fantasy, unresolved conflict."

"So the dream is negotiated."

Natalia looked at him.

"That is one possibility."

"Only one?"

"Negotiation implies participants who know what they are doing. I see no evidence for that."

"Possibly."

"You shouted 'empirical method' first."

"Not the filing cabinet specifically."

"The mistake everyone keeps making is assuming that dream behaviour works like testimony. It does not."

Sherie's eyes shifted toward her.

"A dream image is not a witness under oath. If you dream of killing someone, the dream has not automatically discovered your secret intention to kill them. If you dream of sleeping with someone, the dream has not automatically uncovered a hidden wish to do so."

The image chose sex rather than conversation, dinner, an embrace, a funeral, an argument or simply sitting beside them."

"So you look at why the dream chose that image."
