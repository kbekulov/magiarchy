# The Water Beneath Her Feet

*The River Choir, Part II. Lynleit.*

[Part I: The Empty Boats Beneath the Bridge](../story.html?chapter=the-empty-boats-beneath-the-bridge&version=v4)

## An empty hold

Kyrien was holding a wet board so that I could look underneath it. I remember wishing he would move his fingers. They were close to the edge, and if the boat lurched he would lose the use of them for the rest of the night.

There was water beneath the board. Nothing else that I could see. I touched his wrist, he shifted his hand, and we lowered it together.

Ahead, Reiner had finished inspecting the first boat. Maren and Ivo were checking the second. Nobody had found a person or a cargo. We had waited through four unsuccessful watches to board three empty boats.

“Anything at the controls?” I asked.

“Fixed rudder,” Reiner said.

“Maren?”

“Same.”

Felix asked us to check under the boards. I told him we had. I wanted another answer from somebody, preferably an ordinary one. People sometimes went to considerable trouble to move nothing. There could be a reason for the empty holds. But the tarps had been fastened down. Somebody had finished that work before the boats reached us.

I looked at the part of the tarp still hanging from my hand. The edge had soaked through. I couldn't tell whether that had happened here or farther upstream.

The water was quiet enough to hear it striking the bows. We had passed beyond the bridge lights. On the city side the lamps showed through mist without their posts; on the park side I could still make out branches above the bank. They were much closer than I liked.

“How long until the next bridge?”

Felix gave me the remaining interval. Reiner confirmed that we would be ready to leave.

I had agreed to inspect the traffic, not keep going until I could explain it. I knew that. I was already preparing to tell the others to stop lifting things and face downstream when Kyrien held his breath.

It was the same little pause I had noticed over the cards. I looked at him before he turned towards the trees.

“What?”

“Nothing.”

I didn't think he meant it. He was listening, and I wanted him to tell me what he was listening for. An objection I could examine would have been better than watching his face and trying to manufacture one.

I had asked these people to come. They had accepted the small operation I could describe, with its camera window and exit lines. Reiner's confidence had helped me give the order to descend. It hadn't cured my unease. Now there was nothing in the boats, Kyrien was looking away from the exit, and I was wondering whether I had heard reassurance as an answer to a different question.

Maren spoke before I could ask him again.

“There is something beside us.”

“Do not lean out,” Reiner said.

She had already looked.

Her hip struck the gunwale. I saw her fingers close over the edge, one hand after the other, and her leg disappear below it. The boat tipped, but not enough to account for the way she had moved.

Reiner crossed to her and caught her wrist. She kicked with her free leg, trying to get a knee against the boards. I told Ivo to brace where he was rather than add his weight to their side. He threw the line Reiner called for.

“Something has my legs,” Maren said.

I couldn't see it. I could see what it was doing to her.

I let the tarp fall and pulled the black leather gloves from my belt. The first went on badly. I had to push my fingers to the ends. I rolled that jacket sleeve and the blouse cuff beneath it above my wrist, then did the same with the other sleeve as I pulled on the second glove. Kyrien was gathering the loose line beside me. I told him to keep it clear of his feet.

I didn't use the flame. Reiner's arm was beside Maren's face; Ivo was leaning across them. If I put fire where she was being held without seeing the grip, I could burn the people trying to free her. I needed them apart. I needed a view of what was under the side.

Reiner pulled her shoulder up. For a moment I thought he had her. Then he went forward, both of them struck the water, and Ivo entered after them.

“Felix. Fallback. Now.”

“Moving.”

The line crossed our boat and tightened. Kyrien cut it beside the fitting. I felt the release through my feet, followed by the boat's return in the other direction. I reached for the rail. The gunwale struck my thigh before my hand found it.

I hit the river on my side.

## Where I could still swim

I had shut my mouth. That was the one thing I had done in time.

My jacket filled and pulled across my shoulders. I turned beneath it, opened my eyes and found a pale strip above me. I kicked towards it. There was no pain yet, only cold and the thickness of my own clothes in the water.

My head came out beside the overturned boat. I caught a board, drew a breath and looked for the others.

Reiner had Maren. Ivo was moving towards them. Felix was calling the city bank over the radio. Kyrien surfaced farther from me than I expected. I saw him turn towards the lights.

“City side,” I tried to say.

The water slapped the boat and came over my mouth. I coughed, tightened my hold and tried again. Reiner was already doing it. I didn't need to tell him twice. I needed to get myself clear of the hull before it rolled over me.

I pushed away and started swimming.

I could swim. I had practised because I was afraid of drowning, not because practice had made me stop being afraid. I knew how to turn, how to stop wasting a kick, how to use a breath instead of snatching at the next one. For the first few strokes I was still doing those things.

Then my right ankle stopped.

My body kept moving while my foot didn't. It brought my face down into the water. I drew the leg up, expecting to pull free of something, and felt pressure close around the calf as well.

I got my mouth above water and called for Reiner. I don't know whether he heard. He was occupied with Maren, and between one blink and the next they were farther away.

I kicked at the pressure with my other foot. For an instant I could move again. I turned onto my side to look behind me and saw Kyrien struggling towards the park. He was no longer trying to follow the others.

I wanted to tell him there were steps on the city side. Of course he knew. He had been the one to insist on looking at them. I stopped trying to give him an order he was already unable to follow.

Something closed around my other ankle.

I could still see the hull beside me. I reached for it and got two fingers over its edge. The wood cut into them through the glove. I pulled, but the boat came towards me instead of holding me up. My face went beneath it. I let go before it covered my head.

That was the last choice which felt like one of the swimming exercises I knew.

## Beneath

There were fingers against my boot.

I could see them now. The leather had drawn tight under the grip. A thumb lay along the lacing, and the rest of the hand curved around my ankle. I followed it with my eyes, wanting an arm, a shoulder, something I could strike and understand. There was another hand behind it, then another beneath my knee. Whatever bodies they belonged to were lower than I could see.

I kicked. A wrist bent beneath my heel. It should have made a difference. The fingers didn't open.

My hair floated in front of my face. I swept it away and reached upwards with the same hand, as though clearing my sight might also have brought the surface nearer. The light was still there. I could see the underside of the water moving. Bubbles went up past my fingers and continued after my hand had stopped.

I remember being furious with the bubbles. They had no difficulty going where I wanted to go.

A grip closed higher on my leg. Another caught the jacket at my waist. Its hem pulled tight across my back and then up, bunching beneath my arms. I couldn't bring one arm forward properly. I tried to shrug out of it and felt a pull at the other sleeve.

I had chosen the shorter jacket because of the ropes. I had tested my reach in it on the bridge. Now I couldn't get it off.

My chest wanted me to breathe. I pressed my lips together and pulled at the fabric. There was a hand where the sleeve met the shoulder. I caught its wrist, tried to force it away and saw the fingers dig farther into the suede.

The fear had been with me since childhood. It didn't return as a useful memory. There was no complete scene to look at, no voice giving advice. I wanted somebody above me to notice that I hadn't come up. I wanted a hand that would pull in the other direction.

I reached again. Nothing was close enough.

The gloves made my own hands dark against the reaching ones. I had them on. That was useful. I tried to think about that, about something I had actually managed to do, while another grip found the top of my boot.

I couldn't keep pulling upwards. They were multiplying beneath me, or I was sinking into more of them. I couldn't tell which. The loose parts of my clothes and hair streamed towards the surface while my body went down. I looked along my legs and saw hands at different angles, holding wherever there was room.

One was small enough to close over only part of my wrist when I reached down. I tried to wrench away from that one first. I don't know why its size was worse. It was holding just as hard.

I wanted to hurt them.

The thought was much simpler than anything I had been trying to do. I turned down instead of up and caught the wrist at my knee with both hands.

Blue flame ran between my fingers.

I had expected resistance, a hiss, a waste of the little strength I had left. The flame held its colour in the water. It moved over the wrist beneath my glove, and the fingers opened.

I kicked that leg free. Another hand found it. I put the flame there too.

The light showed me more than I wanted to see. Hands with swollen fingers. A hand whose nails seemed too long because there was so little left around them. Smaller ones reaching between the others. They crowded into the space I was trying to leave.

I couldn't fight them one at a time. I called more flame than I could shape neatly, spread my hands and pushed it through the grips nearest my body. The burning fingers released. Something still held my jacket. I twisted against it until a seam gave, then another. The suede pulled away from my shoulder and disappeared below my elbow.

I didn't try to keep it.

There was space to kick. I used it before I had finished looking. My foot struck a wrist, passed it, struck nothing. I pulled water down with both arms. The flame remained around the hands below me, blue between the moving fingers, and I kept going towards the pale strip above.

I don't know how much of the last stroke was swimming. I remember reaching too soon. I remember my hand still in water when I thought it should have been in air.

Then my face broke through.

I tried to take an entire breath at once and coughed it away. My next breath hurt. I reached out for the boat, for a rope, for anything at the height of my shoulders. There was nothing there.

My palm struck the surface.

It held.

## Above it

I pressed down before I dared think about what I was doing. The water bowed under my hand, deep enough for it to feel as though I was about to slip through. Then the pressure steadied.

I knew that resistance. I had felt it alone at night, where nobody was close enough to see me practising. Knowing it did not make me trust it with hands still moving below my feet.

I brought one knee up. It took too long. My leg shook, and I couldn't get my weight over it without leaning farther onto the hand. I coughed again and almost lost the position. Water ran off my face onto the small hollow beneath my palm.

For a little while I stayed on a hand and a knee, looking down at the place where they should have sunk. I needed both boots beneath me. I didn't want to lift anything that was already holding.

I planted one foot. The surface gave and stopped. I moved the other leg, slowly, and braced with my hand until I was crouching. My wet hair hung across my face. I pushed it back with my free wrist and looked for a bank.

The city lamps were distant and broken by mist. The park trees were nearer. I could see the low branches and the darkness beneath them. There was somewhere to get out. I only had to stand.

Below me, a hand pressed upwards.

I jerked my boot away and almost fell. The hand had not come through. Its fingers spread against the underside of the surface, and the water between us distorted the shape. More hands rose beside it. They reached under my knees and my planted foot, opening and closing without finding me.

I got upright.

My legs wanted to keep kicking. I had to stop them. I was standing above the river with my breath coming in ugly, short catches, and if I let myself behave as though I was still swimming I would lose my balance.

There was a light moving on the city side. I looked at it, then away.

I had kept this ability from everyone. The Church's distinction wasn't between something helpful and something harmful. There were things that were not permitted to look like miracles. I knew enough to practise where nobody watched. I didn't know what I would say if Felix called across the river now and asked me to explain where I was standing.

I wanted him to call anyway. I wanted to hear that he had the others.

Instead, the water in front of me rose around a pale forehead.

At first I thought another person had come up. I took half a step towards her before I saw her eyes.

Fishing line passed through the lids. Little tight stitches, with water caught against the knots. I could see them because her face was turned towards me and she wasn't far away. She didn't blink. There was no opening through which I could see an eye.

I stopped moving.

Her hair lay wet against her cheeks. Water ran from her chin. Beneath it, something silver-grey moved along her chest, and I thought of scales sewn onto cloth. Then one of the shapes turned enough for me to see its mouth.

They were fish. Whole dead fish, overlapping, their bodies making the dress. More emerged as she rose. Their pale mouths opened with the water moving through them. I could see an eye near the waist, another lower down, each facing somewhere different.

I looked back at her face. That was worse than looking at the fish, but at least it was one face.

She said nothing.

“Stay there,” I said.

My voice was rough enough that I wasn't sure she had heard. Her head tilted.

It was a small movement. I had given an order because there was nothing else ready in my mouth. She gave me no sign that it meant anything to her. I lowered my hands a little and waited.

I still had the gloves on. I could call the flame again. I didn't know what it would do to her, how much I could use without losing my footing, or what would happen if she came close enough to touch me. Under the water I had needed the grips to release. Here I had a bank behind me. I could leave.

I glanced down. The hands had followed the space beneath my boots. They were still held below it. I could not see a wall or a skin or anything I could name. I only knew I was above them.

The girl lowered one hand towards the water. Her fingertips touched it, and a small ring spread away.

I watched the ring approach my foot. It passed through the reflection beside it. Nothing struck me. Nothing let go beneath me. I had been holding my breath again, and I let a little of it out.

Her head remained at that angle.

Was she trying to understand what I was standing on? I couldn't even establish whether she was looking at my feet. The stitched lids faced me and gave me nothing to work with.

I took a step backwards.

The water sank slightly beneath the boot and held. I waited until my weight had settled before moving the other foot. The girl stayed where she was.

## The bank behind me

I couldn't walk backwards all the way without looking. The park bank was uneven. A root I couldn't see would be enough to bring me down at the edge, after everything else.

I turned my head, found a pale piece of exposed mud below the branches, and chose it. Then I looked back at the girl.

She hadn't moved closer.

I took another step. Water ran down inside my boots. My blouse stuck to my shoulders where the jacket had been torn away, and the cold on the wet fabric made me want to curl my arms around myself. I kept them out instead, using them for balance.

Something brushed the sole of my right boot from underneath. I felt it only faintly through the yielding support. I put the foot down harder than I meant to and stopped until I was steady.

There was no sense in hurrying if hurrying put me back among them. I repeated that to myself, then hurried the next step anyway. I had to stop a second time.

From the city bank I heard a shout. I couldn't make out the words. I tried to count shapes around the light, but the mist and the distance made it impossible. Reiner had been moving in that direction. Felix had answered me. I held on to those two things and kept going.

The girl was farther away now. That helped. Her dress was becoming one pale shape again instead of dozens of separate bodies. She still hadn't followed.

I watched her while I put the next foot down.

The heel met something solid that didn't yield with the rest of the water. Mud. I nearly stumbled at the change. I bent my knee, felt the slope through the sole and set my other foot beside it.

My hand found a branch.

I squeezed it before I trusted it, then pulled myself beneath the leaves. They struck my wet hair and caught there. I ducked, took another step and got both feet above the waterline.

The slope was steep enough that I needed the branch to keep climbing. I used it. I had no intention of letting go merely because I ought to have been able to walk on land.

When I looked back through the leaves, I couldn't distinguish the girl from the mist. I could still see water moving where she had stood. That didn't tell me whether she had gone under or whether I had lost the angle.

I wanted to sit down. First I had to find somewhere my boots would stay put. I took one more step towards the trees and called Kyrien's name.
