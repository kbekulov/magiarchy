# Chapter 05: The Empty Boats Beneath the Bridge

> Working draft based on the confirmed river operation. Exact dialogue, the oddities seen during the unsuccessful watches, and the missing transition between the river confrontation and Lynleit's arrival on the park bank remain provisional.

## The Fifth Watch

The bridge joined an ordinary street to a place the city had stopped admitting was part of itself.

On the southern bank, apartment windows faced the river. A tram passed at the end of the street every twenty minutes, almost empty at this hour. Lamps threw pale circles across the pavement, and civil safety cameras watched the bridge from both approaches.

On the northern bank, the same bridge ended at a locked path into the quarantined park. No lamps remained lit beyond the barrier. Trees had grown into a single black mass behind it.

Drake's intelligence had identified the river traffic through repetition rather than evidence. Small boats appeared on nights when the sky was darkest. They passed beneath the bridge without navigation lights, visible cargo, or a person at the controls. The pattern suggested preparation and timing. It might also have been contraband, and Drake refused to discard the ordinary explanation simply because it had begun to look strange.

Sherie and Drake had asked Lynleit for a team capable of observing the boats without turning the investigation into an official MSF operation.

The plan was narrow. Felix would freeze the bridge cameras for as long as he could preserve a convincing loop. Reiner would secure the descent ropes. Lynleit, Felix, Reiner, Kyrien, and several trusted officers would board from above, inspect the boats, and leave by ropes at the next bridge. They were not to seize cargo, stop a suspect, or remain after the camera window closed.

In theory, the hardest part was leaving no record that they had been there.

The first watch produced nothing.

The second ended when Felix warned that the camera loop had begun to drift out of time with the traffic lights.

On the third, a branch broke somewhere inside the park before the wind reached the trees. Kyrien turned toward the sound. Lynleit noticed him turning and looked as well. Neither said anything.

On the fourth, one of the frozen camera feeds showed a pale interruption on the park path. Felix blamed compression damage. When he restored the live image, the path was empty.

They had no authority to investigate the park, no safe way to extend the camera window, and no proof that either incident meant anything. Every night ended with the same order from Lynleit.

"Restore the cameras. We leave."

By the fifth watch, nobody expected the boats.

Felix sat inside a maintenance van on the city side with a compact terminal across his knees. The bridge cameras were displayed in four muted panes. Each showed a version of the previous several minutes with no MSF officers in it.

"The loop is clean," he said through the earpieces. "It will not remain clean because time continues to exist despite my objections."

Reiner tested the last anchor point with both hands. "Window."

Felix gave him the remaining time.

"Shorter than yesterday," Reiner said.

"Yesterday had fewer buses. I was not consulted about the timetable."

Lynleit stood against the inner rail and watched the black water below. Her coat concealed the Sparrow carried above her tailbone, but it did nothing against the cold rising from the river. The current caught fragments of city light and stretched them until they broke.

Kyrien waited several paces away. He had no MSF insignia and no recognized place in the formation. Felix had assigned him no task that could not be watched by someone else.

"If they do not appear tonight, we end the pattern," Lynleit said.

"Because the operation has failed?" Kyrien asked.

"Because repeating an intrusion after the target has noticed it is not surveillance."

Felix looked up from the terminal. "That almost sounded directed at someone."

Kyrien did not answer.

Reiner lifted one hand.

The bridge fell silent.

Something had entered the far edge of the river.

At first Lynleit saw only the interruption it made in the reflected lights. Then the first boat separated from the dark, followed by another and another. They were small enough to pass without attracting attention from the embankment. Their lamps were off. Their engines ran at a low, even pressure.

Nobody stood at the controls.

Felix stared at the feed, then at the river itself.

"Those are our boats."

"I can see them," Reiner said.

"There is nobody aboard."

"That is what we are here to establish."

Lynleit watched the distance close. "Positions."

The officers moved.

## The Descent

Reiner went over the rail first. He descended without hurry, controlling the rope with the same severity he applied to every procedure. Two officers followed on the adjacent line. Felix left the terminal running inside the locked van and came down after them with a smaller communications unit fixed against his chest.

Kyrien waited until Lynleit reached the rail.

"You first," he said.

"Is that concern?"

"If I go first, Felix may cut the rope."

Felix's voice arrived through both earpieces. "The possibility has been evaluated."

Lynleit swung over the side.

The river moved beneath her boots. The lead boat passed under the bridge at the expected angle, steady enough that Reiner landed near its center without shifting its course. The others divided themselves between the following boats. Lynleit released the rope last and absorbed the impact through her knees.

Kyrien landed behind her.

The boat continued forward.

There was no pilot.

There was no passenger.

The controls had been left in motion, but no mechanism visible from the deck explained how the vessel had held its course for so long. The interior carried the practical alterations of a boat used to move things without inviting inspection. Tonight there was no cargo to inspect.

Reiner's voice came from the first boat. "Empty."

The second team confirmed the same.

Felix opened the restricted channel. "No people, no visible shipment. Engines active. All boats clean enough to be useless and strange enough to ruin my evening."

Lynleit crouched beside the controls. The metal was wet. So was the deck. Nothing proved that a living person had occupied the boat recently.

The plan had assumed that the boats would carry something. Contraband, documents, weapons, money, a courier, even a body would have given the operation a recognizable shape. An empty boat was not an answer. It was the removal of the question they had prepared to ask.

The park passed on their right. From the water, the trees appeared closer than they had from the bridge. Their lowest branches extended over the bank and hid the path behind them.

Kyrien looked into the trees.

"What?" Lynleit asked.

"Nothing."

"You keep finding it."

"It keeps being there."

The nearest officer shifted toward the gunwale. "There is something beside us."

Reiner answered at once. "Do not lean out."

She had already looked down.

Her right leg vanished over the side.

There was no splash large enough to explain the force. Her hip struck the gunwale, both hands caught the rail, and the rest of her body followed as if the river had taken hold of her boot.

Reiner crossed from the lead boat before anyone else moved. He caught her wrist. For a moment his weight held her above the surface.

Then his shoulder jerked downward.

"Line," he ordered.

Felix threw it. Another officer braced behind him. The boats had begun to separate, not quickly, but in different directions. The rope snapped tight between them.

The woman in the water tried to kick toward the hull.

Her face changed.

"Something has my legs."

Reiner pulled hard enough to bring her shoulder against the side. The water pulled harder. His footing broke, and he went in beside her.

The river ceased to behave like water shared by bodies of different weight. It took each person according to a separate direction.

One officer entered to reach Reiner. Felix followed the line toward them and was dragged from the deck when it twisted around his arm. Kyrien cut the slack before it could take Lynleit with it, but the boat rolled beneath both of them. The gunwale struck her thigh. A moment later the deck was gone from under her feet.

Cold closed around the team.

## Two Shores

The city bank was the assigned emergency route. It had steps, lamps, and streets where an extraction vehicle could approach without crossing the quarantine.

Reiner forced the first officer toward it. When something pulled her legs down, he locked an arm under her shoulders and drove forward with both of his. Another officer reached them from the side. Together they gained a metre, lost half of it, and gained it again.

Felix kept the communications channel open between breaths.

"City side. Do not fight for the boats. City side."

The order gave the officers one direction when the river seemed determined to give each of them another.

Hands caught at boots, ankles, and calves. Most of the officers felt only pressure and fingers where no person could remain submerged. They kicked free without seeing what they struck.

Reiner reached the embankment first and lifted the female officer high enough for Felix to catch her from the steps. The others followed in fragments, coughing river water and pulling one another beyond the edge.

"It was hands," the woman said.

Felix knelt beside her. "What was?"

"On my legs. Someone was holding my legs."

Reiner looked back at the river.

Lynleit was not among them.

Neither was Kyrien.

The force beneath the water had turned Kyrien away from the city bank. He stopped spending strength on a direction the river would not permit and chose the nearer ground. The park bank offered mud, exposed roots, and a wall of unlit trees. He caught the lowest root with both hands and pulled himself out beneath the branches.

The quarantine waited behind him.

He remained low and moved inside the tree line. From there, he could see the broken formation of boats and the heads of the officers reaching the opposite shore.

He could not see Lynleit.

Then the river struck the underside of the surface from below.

## Beneath

Lynleit knew how to swim.

That knowledge survived for the first several seconds. She turned toward the nearest light, drove one arm forward, and tried to bring her legs behind her.

Her right ankle stopped.

Fingers closed around it.

Another hand caught her calf. A third pressed against the back of her knee. They did not seize her in one direction. They climbed.

She kicked and felt a wrist bend beneath her heel. The grip did not release. Her coat opened in the current and made every movement heavier.

The surface remained visible above her, close enough to promise air and far enough to refuse it.

Her fear of drowning had existed since childhood. Training had given it procedures: control the breath, identify the current, discard weight, choose a direction. The hands made each instruction useless. They were not a current. They did not tire. They had no single body against which leverage mattered.

One closed around her other ankle.

The river pulled her farther from the light.

Panic arrived without confusion. It was clean, furious, and complete.

Magecraft answered it before restraint could.

Lynleit twisted downward instead of up. She drove both hands into the dark mass beneath her and released every reserve her body had gathered for survival. The water convulsed around her. Fingers opened. Others caught at her sleeves, her boots, the edge of her coat.

She tore upward through them.

Her head broke the surface. Air entered as pain. She coughed, reached for anything that might hold her, and found nothing.

Her foot came down.

The river held it.

Lynleit rose with the movement, one knee bent, both arms spread for balance. Water ran from her hair across her face. Her breath would not slow. She looked down because she did not yet understand why she was no longer sinking.

Hands filled the darkness beneath her.

Some were pale. Some had lost the shape of living hands. Small ones reached between larger ones. They opened and closed beneath the soles of her boots, pressing against the underside of the surface.

None passed through it.

Lynleit stood above them.

From the trees, Kyrien watched without moving.

The water ahead of Lynleit swelled.

Hair appeared first, flattened against a white forehead. Then came a face with fishing line drawn through both eyelids. The knots were neat. Water moved from them like tears that could not begin or end.

The girl rose until the river released the upper half of her dress. Dead fish had been fastened together in overlapping rows, their bodies colourless, their mouths opening as the current passed through them.

She faced Lynleit.

The drowned hands continued reaching below.

The girl said nothing.

Lynleit could not tell whether sewn eyes were capable of looking at her, but she felt the attention settle. The girl's head tilted slowly to one side.

The gesture carried no threat. It was bewilderment.

People entered the river. The hands caught them. The river received them.

Lynleit had entered and remained above it.

The girl did not understand.

Neither did Lynleit.

## The Park Bank

Later, Lynleit reached the quarantined side of the river.

Kyrien found her beyond the lowest branches, where the bank rose into the trees. Water ran from her coat and collected beneath her boots. Her ash-black hair clung to her cheeks and throat. She was still breathing as if the hands remained around her legs.

He removed his jacket. It was as wet as everything else and therefore useless.

"A thoughtful gesture," Lynleit said.

"I reconsidered it."

"Sensibly."

They moved deeper into the trees until the river could no longer be seen between the trunks. The city bank remained visible in pieces across the water. Felix and Reiner were gathering the officers beneath the bridge lights. On this side, the quarantine swallowed sound before it travelled far.

Lynleit stopped at the base of a broad tree and sat. Kyrien lowered himself behind her. They settled back to back, shoulders touching through soaked clothes, each lending the other the little warmth that remained.

For a while neither spoke.

"You came ashore on this side," Lynleit said.

"It was closer."

"So you could see the river."

"Parts of it."

She waited.

Kyrien did not fill silence merely because somebody else had made room for him.

"Did you see anything strange?" she asked.

"The boats had no crews. Something tried to drown all of us. We are sitting inside a quarantined park in wet clothes."

"That is not what I asked."

"It is what happened."

Lynleit turned her face slightly, not enough to look at him. "And nothing else?"

"Nothing I can explain."

It was a careful answer. It allowed the entire night to remain true without giving her the one fact she was searching for.

She could have pressed him. She could have asked where he had stood, what direction he had faced, or when he had last seen her in the water. Every question would tell him that there was something she needed hidden.

Lynleit let her head rest back against his shoulder.

"Then do not invent an explanation," she said.

"I was not planning to."

Across the river, Felix's light moved along the embankment. Reiner stood at the edge, looking toward the park.

Kyrien saw the search before Lynleit did. He remained still for another moment, keeping his back against hers while her breathing slowed.

He had entered her service because she left him no acceptable alternative.

She had not ordered his silence. Keeping the river to himself was the first decision about her that belonged entirely to him.
