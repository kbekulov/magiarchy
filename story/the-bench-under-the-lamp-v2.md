## The Bench under the Lamp

The path divided again.

Lynleit stopped at the fork and looked back. Kyrien was looking back too.

"That is unhelpful," she said.

"I didn't put it there."

They had finished work before the light began to fail. The park still lay between them and a sensible end to the evening. Neither had been particularly diligent about finding the shortest way through it.

Kyrien took out his flask. One small drink, then the cap went back on. Lynleit waited while he returned it to the same pocket.

"Medicine?"

"Long day."

She chose the left path. He followed without offering a better one.

After a while, a lamppost appeared between the trees. Beneath it stood a bench with a dry seat. Kyrien sat down.

"We've established one thing," Lynleit said.

"That you chose left?"

"That this path has a bench."

He moved his elbow. She sat beside him, closer than the available space required, and leaned back. Beyond the light, the path continued into trees they had already spent too much of the day looking at.

Her shoulder rested against his. Neither moved it.

"You could admit you don't know the way," he said.

"I know several ways. I'm choosing between them."

"You've tried that answer before."

She turned her head.

"When?"

He looked at her for a moment.

"The evening you wouldn't explain anything."

"You'll have to be considerably more specific."

"The curse."

Lynleit studied a loose thread at her cuff. The curse had been gone for months. His memory, unfortunately, had made a complete recovery too.

"We survived."

"I noticed."

"Then your complaint seems poorly founded."

"It was a question."

She let her hand fall between them. He glanced at it, then back at her face.

There had been quite a lot of reading since that evening. Some of it had been useful. Some had disagreed so confidently with the rest that she had wanted to mark it and send it back.

None of it had suggested a park bench.

"What?" Kyrien asked.

"Nothing."

"You're looking at me."

"You are very near."

"You sat there."

She shifted along the bench and rested her head in his lap. Above her, Kyrien looked down without finding anything to say.

For once, she let him take his time.

### Afterward

They remained beneath the lamp a little longer.

Lynleit straightened her cuff. Kyrien watched her with an expression she would have liked to preserve for later reference.

"Research was successful," she said. "Thank you for your participation."

He looked down at the bench, then at her.

"Research."

"You're welcome."

She stood and checked that she had everything. He took a little longer to get up.

"We should probably determine where we are."

"Now?"

"You were the one who stopped walking."

They left the lamp behind.

For several minutes Lynleit was excellent company. She did not object when he chose a turning. She even allowed him to finish a sentence about their route.

"Is this how you're going to win arguments from now on?"

"You wish. Not a chance."

He made no attempt to contest the result.

They walked farther. Lynleit swallowed, frowned briefly, and continued. A few steps later the frown returned.

She stopped.

"Give me your whiskey."

"Why?"

"You don't need to know."

His hand went to the familiar pocket. He passed her the flask without another question.

She drank, screwed the cap back on, and returned it.

"This way," she said.

Kyrien followed.
