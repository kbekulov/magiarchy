Territorial order
World/The Magiarchy

Territorial magical order

A hierarchy that
ends at the border

The highest official position a Magus can obtain is Magiarch. Each Magiarch oversees magical affairs and relations with the churches inside a defined territory, but no Magiarch may turn that authority into a larger united organization.

01

Highest office

The Magiarch is the ceiling of lawful magical authority

OFFICIAL APEXMagiarch

The office is usually held by the main Magus assigned to oversee the Magiarchy within a single territorial jurisdiction.

01
Territorial oversight

Authority is tied to a defined country or district rather than to a universal magical hierarchy.

02
Church relations

The office manages the local relationship between organized Magi and the churches that monitor and counterbalance them.

03
Factional leadership

A Magiarch and the Magi aligned with them become a political actor with interests, allies, rivals, and local pressures.

02

Territorial scale

Jurisdiction follows practical size

COMMON MODEL

One country

A single Magiarch may oversee the whole territory when its size and magical population can be managed as one jurisdiction.

LARGER TERRITORY

One district

A large country may be divided into several Magiarchates, each governed within its own district rather than subordinated to a national magical superior.

03

Structural counterbalance

Magic is fragmented because the churches are not

MAGIARCHATES

Territorial and separate

Magiarchs are explicitly forbidden from uniting into a larger organization. Their lawful authority stops with their territory.

COUNTERWEIGHT
CHURCHES

Organized across borders

The churches retain cross-national organization, giving them a wider structure that counterbalances the concentrated power of magic users.

WHY THE BAN EXISTS

A united organization of Magiarchs would overturn the intended balance and create open conflict with the churches.

04

Relations without a sovereign

Magiarchates exist in an anarchic order

NO HIGHER MAGICAL GOVERNMENT
There is no lawful authority above the Magiarchs capable of reliably settling disputes between them.

Anarchy here does not mean constant chaos. It means the absence of a superior power that can compel every Magiarchate to obey.

01Self-preservation

Each faction must judge threats and protect its own position.

02Alliance

Cooperation depends on interest, trust, and temporary alignment.

03Rivalry

Competing aims can produce pressure resembling conflict between states.

04Uncertainty

No Magiarch can be certain how another faction will use its power.

PLAUSIBLE METHOD · NOT CONFIRMED

Institutions may penetrate one another

Rival Magiarchates, criminal Magi, and church networks may covertly or illegally place operatives, informants, or influence inside one another's structures. If this occurs, visible affiliation cannot be treated as proof of who truly directed an action.

05

Outside the sanctioned order

A Magus without jurisdiction becomes a common enemy

CRIMINAL STATUSUnbound and unsanctioned

A Magus is treated as a criminal when they belong to no territorial jurisdiction and practice outside church supervision or permission.

  1. 01
    No jurisdiction

    The Magus does not belong to a recognized Magiarchate.

  2. 02
    No church sanction

    Their practice proceeds without church supervision or permission.

CHURCH RESPONSE

Special envoys

The churches use their covert operatives to identify, pursue, and suppress Magi outside the sanctioned order.

SHARED TARGET
MAGIARCH RESPONSE

Territorial resources

The responsible Magiarch is expected to use the resources under their authority to hunt the same offender.

UNEASY COOPERATION

The pursuit is shared, but trust is not. Church authorities often doubt that a Magiarch will act decisively enough against another Magus.
